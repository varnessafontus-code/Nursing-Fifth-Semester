Lab Values & Diagnostics Reference

Adult and pediatric normal ranges by system, plus a diagnostic-test rundown — built for bedside/exam recall. Always confirm against your facility's reference range, since assay methods vary by lab.

NB Neonate 0–28d INF Infant 1–12mo Child 1–12y Adol 12–18y Normal High/Low alert Critical/panic
🚨 Highest-yield panic values — call the provider
Glucose<40 or >400 mg/dL (tighter in neonates — <40 is already critical in a newborn)
Potassium<2.5 or >6.5 mEq/L — cardiac arrhythmia risk
Sodium<120 or >160 mEq/L — seizure risk at extremes
Hemoglobin<5 or >20 g/dL

Illustrative thresholds for prioritization practice — actual panic values are set by each lab and vary by age/context.

CBC

Complete Blood Count

Newborns run high (placental transfer, birth dehydration); infants dip during physiologic anemia around 2–3 months, then rise toward adult range through adolescence.

WBC×10³/mm³
Adult5–10
PedsNB 9–30 · INF 6–17.5 · Child 5–13.5
⚠ <2 or >30

Trends up with infection/stress; down with marrow suppression or overwhelming sepsis.

RBC×10⁶/mm³
AdultM 4.7–6.1 · F 4.2–5.4
PedsNB 4.8–7.1 · INF 3.9–5.5 · Child 4.0–5.5

Rarely interpreted alone — pair with Hgb/Hct.

Hemoglobing/dL
AdultM 14–18 · F 12–16
PedsNB 14–24 · INF nadir 9–11 · Child 11–16
⚠ <5 or >20

The 2–3 month nadir is normal physiologic anemia of infancy — not automatically pathologic.

Hematocrit%
AdultM 42–52 · F 37–47
PedsNB 44–64 · INF 32–42 · Child 33–42
⚠ <15 or >60

Roughly 3× the hemoglobin ("rule of 3") when drawn together.

Platelets×10³/mm³
Adult150–400
PedsSimilar all ages, ~150–450
⚠ <20 or >1,000

<50k → bleeding precautions; <20k → spontaneous bleed risk.

BMP/CMP

Electrolytes, Renal & Glucose

Immature kidneys mean infants can't concentrate urine or buffer acid as well as adults — expect mildly lower BUN/creatinine and a narrower buffering margin.

SodiummEq/L
Adult135–145
PedsNB 133–146; otherwise same as adult
⚠ <120 or >160

Correct slowly — rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia risks osmotic demyelination.

PotassiummEq/L
Adult3.5–5.0
PedsNB 3.7–5.9 · INF 4.1–5.3 · Child 3.4–4.7
⚠ <2.5 or >6.5

Kids normally run higher than adult upper limits — don't panic at 5.3 in an infant.

ChloridemEq/L
Adult98–106
PedsSimilar; NB 96–106

Moves with sodium; check alongside acid-base status.

CO₂ (bicarb)mEq/L
Adult23–29
PedsSlightly lower: 20–28
⚠ <15 or >40

Reflects the metabolic component of acid-base balance.

BUNmg/dL
Adult10–20
PedsInfant/child 5–18; lower muscle mass
⚠ >100

Rises with dehydration and GI bleed, independent of renal function.

Creatininemg/dL
AdultM 0.6–1.2 · F 0.5–1.1
PedsNB 0.3–1.0 · Infant/Child 0.2–0.7

Rises steadily with muscle mass — a "normal adult" value in a toddler is actually high.

Glucosemg/dL
Adult70–110 fasting
PedsNB 40–60 (24h) then 50–90; Child 60–100
⚠ <40 or >400

Neonates burn glycogen fast — screen at-risk newborns within hours of birth.

Calcium (total)mg/dL
Adult8.5–10.5
PedsNB 7.0–9.5 · Child 8.8–10.8
⚠ <6 or >13

Watch for Chvostek's/Trousseau's signs with hypocalcemia.

Magnesiummg/dL
Adult1.5–2.5
PedsSimilar; NB slightly higher 1.4–2.9
⚠ <1.0 or >4.0

Low Mg travels with low K⁺/Ca²⁺ — replace Mg first or the others won't correct.

Phosphorusmg/dL
Adult2.5–4.5
PedsINF 4.5–6.5 · Child 4.0–5.5 · Adol 3.5–5.0
⚠ <1 or >8

Runs high in growing kids from active bone turnover — expected, not pathologic.

LFT

Liver Function

Alk phos runs high in children simply from bone growth — check it against the bilirubin/AST/ALT pattern before assuming hepatic pathology.

ASTU/L
Adult10–40
PedsNB up to ~58; child similar to adult

Not liver-specific — also elevated with muscle/cardiac injury.

ALTU/L
Adult7–56
PedsSimilar, mildly higher in infancy

More liver-specific than AST.

Alk PhosU/L
Adult44–147
PedsChild 100–350+ (growth spurts spike further)

High Alk Phos + normal bili/AST/ALT in a kid = bone growth, not liver disease.

Total bilirubinmg/dL
Adult0.1–1.2
PedsNB peaks ~5–6 (day 3–5); hour-specific nomogram applies
⚠ Rapid rise/high per nomogram

Jaundice in the first 24h of life is never normal.

Direct (conjugated) bilimg/dL
Adult<0.3
PedsSame — should stay low at any age
⚠ Any elevation abnormal

Elevated direct bili always warrants work-up.

Albuming/dL
Adult3.5–5.0
PedsNB 2.6–3.6; rises to adult range in childhood
⚠ <1.5

Low albumin → edema risk; also tracks nutrition/chronic illness.

COAG

Coagulation Studies

Clotting factors are physiologically immature at birth, so neonatal PT/aPTT run longer than adult values without indicating a bleeding disorder. Therapy targets for warfarin and heparin are included alongside the baseline values they're monitored with.

PTseconds
Adult11–12.5
PedsNB mildly prolonged, 13–20 first days
⚠ >20 (non-NB)

Reflects the extrinsic pathway; monitors warfarin via INR.

INR
Adult0.8–1.1
PedsSame reference at any age
⚠ >5.0 unintended

Therapeutic anticoag target is usually 2–3 — not the "normal" range.

💊 Warfarin (Coumadin)therapy target
StandardINR 2.0–3.0 (AFib, DVT/PE)
Mech. valveINR 2.5–3.5
⚠ INR >5 unintended = high bleed risk

Antidote: vitamin K (phytonadione). Watch for interactions — vitamin K-rich greens, antibiotics, NSAIDs/aspirin all shift INR.

aPTTseconds
Adult30–40
PedsNB prolonged, 30–55+
⚠ >70

Reflects the intrinsic pathway; monitors unfractionated heparin.

💉 Heparin (IV, unfractionated)therapy target
TargetaPTT 1.5–2.5× control (~60–80 sec, lab-dependent)
LMWHe.g. enoxaparin — no routine aPTT; anti-Xa if monitored
⚠ aPTT >100 sec = high bleed risk

Antidote: protamine sulfate. Watch platelets for HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia).

Fibrinogenmg/dL
Adult200–400
PedsNB slightly lower, 150–300
⚠ <100

Falling fibrinogen + rising PT/PTT is a DIC red flag.

CARDIAC

Cardiac Markers

Used mainly in adults for ACS; in pediatrics reserved for suspected myocarditis, Kawasaki disease, or congenital heart failure.

Troponin Ing/mL
Adult<0.04
PedsSame cutoff; used selectively
⚠ Any detectable rise

Peaks ~24h, stays elevated 7–10 days — most specific marker available.

BNPpg/mL
Adult<100
PedsHigher baseline in neonates; age-specific ranges
⚠ >900

Rises with ventricular stretch/volume overload — tracks HF severity.

CK-MB
Adult<5% of total CK
PedsSimilar

Largely replaced by troponin, still tests on some exams.

Adult<100

HDLmg/dL

THYROID

Thyroid Function

A normal neonatal TSH surge in the first 24–48 hours is expected — that's exactly why newborn screening is timed after that window.

TSHmIU/L
Adult0.4–4.0
PedsNB surge 20–40 (first 24–48h), then normalizes

Congenital hypothyroidism screening relies on this surge resolving normally.

Free T4ng/dL
Adult0.8–1.8
PedsNB higher, 2.0–5.0, declining through infancy

Untreated congenital hypothyroidism causes irreversible intellectual disability.

ABG

Arterial Blood Gases

Fetal-to-newborn transition has its own "normal" in the first minutes-to-hours of life — don't apply adult O₂ targets to a delivery room baby.

pH
Adult7.35–7.45
PedsNB 7.29–7.45 acceptable day 1
⚠ <7.2 or >7.6

<7.35 = acidosis; >7.45 = alkalosis. Read pH, then CO₂, then HCO₃.

PaCO₂mmHg
Adult35–45
PedsSame across ages
⚠ <20 or >70

Respiratory component — high = respiratory acidosis.

PaO₂mmHg
Adult80–100
PedsNB transitional 60–70, rises within days
⚠ <40

Don't chase adult PaO₂ targets in the first minutes of life.

HCO₃⁻mEq/L
Adult22–26
PedsInfants lower baseline, 19–24
⚠ <10 or >40

Metabolic component — low = met. acidosis, high = met. alkalosis.

O₂ saturation%
Adult>95
PedsNB rises 85→95%+ over first ~10 min
⚠ <85 sustained

Congenital heart disease can shift the "acceptable" target.

UA

Urinalysis

Infant kidneys can't concentrate urine as well as an adult's — a lower max specific gravity in a baby is expected, not a red flag on its own.

Specific gravity
Adult1.005–1.030
PedsInfant ceiling lower, ~1.005–1.020

High = concentrated/dehydrated; low = dilute or impaired concentrating ability.

pH
Adult4.6–8.0 (avg ~6.0)
PedsSame at any age

Diet-dependent; cranberry juice or high-protein diet acidifies urine.

Protein / Glucose / Ketones
AdultNegative
PedsSame

Kids ketose faster than adults during illness/fasting (lower glycogen reserves).

WBC / RBCper hpf
AdultWBC <5 · RBC <2–3
PedsSame

Elevated WBC = pyuria; rule out contamination before RBC work-up.

DX

Common Diagnostic Tests

What each test evaluates and the nursing consideration most likely to show up on an exam.

🩻 Chest X-ray

Evaluates: lung fields, heart size, tube/line placement. Nursing: remove metal/jewelry from the field; confirm pregnancy status if relevant.

🖥️ CT (with contrast)

Evaluates: detailed cross-sectional imaging. Nursing: check renal function and iodine/shellfish allergy first; NPO per protocol.

🧲 MRI

Evaluates: soft tissue, neuro, cardiac detail without radiation. Nursing: screen for implanted metal/pacemakers; young kids often need sedation (NPO).

📡 Ultrasound

Evaluates: soft tissue, abdominal organs, fetal/cardiac structures. Nursing: no radiation — full bladder needed for pelvic studies.

❤️ ECG/EKG

Evaluates: cardiac electrical activity/rhythm. Nursing: non-invasive; lead placement accuracy matters for interpretation.

🧠 EEG

Evaluates: brain electrical activity, seizure focus. Nursing: sleep deprivation may be requested beforehand; wash hair after (electrode gel).

💉 Lumbar puncture

Evaluates: CSF — infection, bleeding, pressure. Nursing: tight fetal/side-lying position; monitor for post-LP headache; strict asepsis.

🔦 Endoscopy / colonoscopy

Evaluates: direct GI mucosa visualization, biopsy capability. Nursing: bowel prep, NPO, consent; monitor airway during sedation.

❤️‍🩹 Cardiac catheterization

Evaluates: coronary/chamber pressures, congenital defects. Nursing: check distal pulses pre/post; monitor site for bleeding.

🧫 Biopsy

Evaluates: tissue diagnosis (liver/renal/marrow). Nursing: check coags beforehand; monitor for bleeding/pain after.

🩸 Blood cultures

Evaluates: bacteremia/sepsis source. Nursing: draw before antibiotics; strict sterile technique.

🫁 Pulmonary function tests

Evaluates: lung volumes, airflow pattern. Nursing: requires cooperation with forced breathing — limited use in young kids.

🧪 PPD / Mantoux

Evaluates: TB exposure. Nursing: read induration (not redness) at 48–72h; document in mm.

🍼 Newborn heel-stick screen

Evaluates: metabolic/genetic/endocrine disorders (PKU, congenital hypothyroidism). Nursing: time after 24h of life + adequate feeding.

⭐ Apgar score

Evaluates: immediate newborn transition (quick assessment, not a lab). Nursing: score at 1 & 5 min — HR, resp effort, tone, reflex irritability, color.

Built as a study/recall aid — not a clinical reference for patient care decisions. Reference ranges vary by lab, assay, and textbook (Wong's, Iggy, Hurst, etc. don't always agree to the decimal); confirm against your program's assigned source before an exam.
