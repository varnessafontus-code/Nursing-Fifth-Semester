Where Klimek and Iggy differ
  1. Asystole drugs. Klimek says "epinephrine and atropine." Atropine is no longer used for asystole in current advanced cardiac life support; Iggy lists cardiopulmonary resuscitation and epinephrine and says do not defibrillate. Atropine belongs with symptomatic bradycardia.
  2. Ventricular fibrillation first step. Klimek's sample question picks chest compressions. Iggy says defibrillate immediately when a defibrillator is ready, and start compressions if it is not readily available. Read the stem: no defibrillator in the room yet means compressions.
  3. Premature ventricular complexes after a myocardial infarction. Klimek calls them low priority unless there are 6 or more a minute, runs, or R-on-T. Iggy calls them a possible warning of ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation and says to check potassium, magnesium, and perfusion. For your NUR 205 exam, go with Iggy.
