Hurst rule Limit protein in kidney disease except nephrotic syndrome.

Acute kidney injury

  • Types Prerenal = no blood to the kidney (shock, hypovolemia, low cardiac output) · Intrarenal = damage inside (glomerulonephritis (GN), contrast, aminoglycosides, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) (Iggy: NSAIDs = prerenal)) · Postrenal = can't get out (prostate, stones, clotted catheter).
  • Phases Initiation → oliguric (output falls, can reach 100 mL/24 hr or less; about 10–14 days) (Iggy: oliguria = under 400 mL/day; 100 or less = anuria) → diuretic (large output; replace fluid and electrolytes) → recovery (3–12 months).
  • Signs Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine up · fixed specific gravity · fluid overload (hypertension (HTN), crackles) · uremia (nausea and vomiting (N/V), confusion, itching) · phosphorus up, calcium down · anemia · high potassium (K) · metabolic acidosis.
  • High K first Intravenous (IV) calcium gluconate (protects the heart, doesn't lower potassium (K)) → IV insulin + glucose (shifts K in) → K binder (removes K) → diuretic or dialysis.
  • Nursing Hourly output, daily weight (1 kg = 1,000 mL), fluid = output + 500 mL, smallest intravenous (IV) volumes, no nephrotoxins, infection prevention, bed rest.
  • Diet High carbs and fats; low protein (0.6 g/kg; 1–1.5 g/kg on dialysis); low potassium (K) and phosphate.
  • Contrast Check creatinine first; hydrate before and after; hold metformin, restart after 48 hr only if creatinine is normal.

Kidney stones

  • Signs Severe flank pain, nausea and vomiting (N/V), hematuria.
  • Treat Pain meds right away · ondansetron · tamsulosin (relaxes the ureter) · increase fluids · strain all urine. After lithotripsy, sand-like sediment = it worked.
  • 24-hour urine Discard the first void and start the clock · save everything · keep the last void · miss one = start over.

Kid version

Kidneys are coffee filters. Prerenal: no water poured in. Intrarenal: the filter is torn. Postrenal: the cup underneath is blocked. Nephrotic syndrome is a filter with big holes, so the "sponge" protein that holds water in your blood leaks out and you puff up everywhere.

Cardiovascular

Hurst pp. 79–100 · Iggy

Cardiac output basics

  • Terms Preload = blood returning and stretch · afterload = resistance the left ventricle pumps against (hypertension (HTN) raises it → heart failure (HF)) · cardiac output (CO) = heart rate (HR) × stroke volume.
  • Low CO Level of consciousness (LOC) down · chest pain · crackles · cool, clammy skin · output under 30 mL/hr · weak pulses.
  • Drugs Lower preload: diuretics, nitrates · lower afterload: angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), hydralazine · contractility: dobutamine, milrinone, digoxin · rate: beta blockers, calcium channel blockers.
  • No output Pulseless ventricular tachycardia (VT), ventricular fibrillation (VF), asystole → cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). VF: defibrillate first, then epinephrine; amiodarone or lidocaine if it persists.

Stable angina vs. acute coronary syndrome

Stable anginaAcute coronary syndrome (ACS) (unstable angina, myocardial infarction (MI))
TriggerExertion, cold, big mealsCan happen at rest
ReliefRest or nitroglycerin (NTG)Not relieved; unstable angina = impending myocardial infarction (MI)
TroponinNormalUp in myocardial infarction (MI)
  • ACS signs Crushing pressure radiating to jaw, arm, back; sweating, nausea and vomiting (N/V). Women: indigestion, fatigue, can't catch their breath. Older adults: shortness of breath. Diabetics and women delay care.
  • ED 12-lead within 10 min · oxygen (O₂) only if oxygen saturation (SpO₂) under 90% · chew aspirin · nitroglycerin (NTG) (if systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 100, no phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) drug) · morphine if pain persists · head of bed (HOB) up. ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) → percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) within 90 min.
  • NTG 1 sublingual (SL) every 5 min × 3; sit first. Home: 1 tablet → not better in 5 min → call 911. Expect headache, blood pressure (BP) drop. Dark glass bottle; replace tablets every 6 months (Iggy: 3–5 months). No sildenafil or tadalafil within 24–48 hr. Patch off 12–14 hr a day.
  • Other drugs Beta blockers (hold if heart rate (HR) under 50–60 or systolic blood pressure (SBP) under 100) · calcium channel blockers · statin for every acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patient.
  • Teach Rest, small low-fat meals, no isometric exercise, stop smoking, nitroglycerin (NTG) before exertion, wait 2 hr after eating to exercise.

Cath, reperfusion, and coronary artery bypass graft (CABG)

  • Cath before Allergy to iodine/shellfish (Iggy: ask about prior contrast reactions instead) · kidney function · hold metformin · mark pulses.
  • Cath after Site bleeding/hematoma (major complication) · 5 Ps · leg straight, flat 4–6 hr (Iggy: 2–6 hr, head of bed (HOB) up to 30°) · report site pain.
  • Fibrinolytics Alteplase, tenecteplase: door-to-drug 30 min, within 12 hr of onset. Major risk = bleeding; new headache or confusion = brain bleed. No intramuscular injections (IMs) or arterial blood gases (ABGs). Worked = pain stops, reperfusion dysrhythmias, ST normalizes.
  • After PCI Chest pain = reocclusion → call at once.
  • After CABG Chest tube over 150 mL/hr → report · heavy drainage that suddenly stops → tamponade · atelectasis is #1 · atrial fibrillation (A-fib) on days 2–3.
  • Rehab Walking, gradual activity, no straining (stool softener), sex in about 1 week to 10 days.

Heart failure

Left = LungsRight = the Rest of the body
Crackles, dyspnea, orthopnea, pink frothy sputum, third heart sound (S3), restlessnessJugular venous distention (JVD), dependent edema, ascites, big liver, weight gain
  • Tests B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) (turn nesiritide off 2 hr before drawing) · echo (ejection fraction) · New York Heart Association (NYHA) class I–intravenous (IV).
  • Drugs Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor = drug of choice (cough, high potassium (K), first-dose hypotension; never with an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB)) · beta blocker (start low, go slow) · loop diuretic in the morning (low K; intravenous (IV) push over 1–2 min) · spironolactone (high K, no salt substitutes).
  • Digoxin Apical pulse 1 full min; hold if under 60. Level 0.5–2.0. Toxicity: early nausea and vomiting (N/V), anorexia; late vision changes, dysrhythmias. Low potassium (K) or magnesium (Mg) → toxicity.
  • Teach Weigh daily, same time and scale; report 2–3 lb in a day (Iggy: 1–2 lb overnight or 3 lb in a week) · low sodium · watch canned food and over-the-counter (OTC) drugs · report shortness of breath (SOB), swelling, cough.

Pacemakers

  • Rule Rate may go above the set rate, never below. Loss of capture = spike with no beat; failure to sense = fires at the wrong time.
  • After Most common complication = lead displacement: don't raise that arm over the shoulder (Iggy: and no lifting over 10 lb, about 4 weeks).
  • Teach Pulse daily · identification (ID) card · avoid strong magnets, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) · phone on the opposite side · household appliances are fine.

Kid version

The heart is two pumps. When the left one gets tired, blood backs up into the lungs (Left = Lungs). When the right one gets tired, it backs up into the body: puffy legs and big neck veins. Tamponade is the heart squeezed by a water balloon so it can't fill.

Gastrointestinal

Hurst pp. 142–158 · Iggy

Pancreatitis

  • What Enzymes digest the pancreas. Causes: #1 gallstones, #2 alcohol.
  • Signs Severe epigastric pain to the back, worse with eating · rigid, board-like abdomen · Cullen (navel) and Grey Turner (flank) bruising = bleeding · low blood pressure (BP).
  • Labs Lipase (most specific) and amylase up · glucose up · hemoglobin and hematocrit (H&H) down = bleeding, up = dehydration · calcium may drop.
  • Treat Pain control · keep the stomach "empty and dry": nothing by mouth (NPO), nasogastric (NG) to suction, bed rest (Iggy: NPO + intravenous (IV) fluids for all; NG only for severe vomiting or ileus) · proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), histamine-2 (H2) blockers · insulin.
  • Chronic Pancreatic enzymes with meals; no alcohol, smoking, or high-fat meals.

Cirrhosis, encephalopathy, and varices

  • Liver's jobs Clotting factors, albumin, drug breakdown, ammonia → urea. Scarring → portal hypertension → ascites, big spleen, varices.
  • Hurst rules Sick liver = bleeding is concern #1 · lower drug doses · no acetaminophen · avoid narcotics and sedatives.
  • Care Daily weight and girth · low sodium · less protein (Iggy: moderate protein) · bleeding precautions · no alcohol.
  • Procedures Liver biopsy: supine, right arm up, exhale and hold → after, lie on the right side. Paracentesis: void first, sit up; watch for shock.
  • Encephalopathy Ammonia up → confusion, asterixis, fetor, handwriting changes. Triggers: gastrointestinal (GI) bleed, constipation, low potassium (K), infection, sedatives.
  • Lactulose Goal 2–3 soft stools a day; too many → low potassium (K) and dehydration. Rifaximin kills ammonia-making bacteria.
  • Bleeding varices Alcohol use + gastrointestinal (GI) bleed = varices until proven otherwise. Airway, breathing, circulation (ABCs), oxygen (O₂), 2 large-bore intravenous lines (IVs), blood, octreotide, banding first-line, balloon tamponade 12 hr max (airway emergency → deflate and remove), beta blockers to prevent rebleeding.

Hiatal hernia vs. dumping syndrome

Hiatal hernia: keep the stomach DOWNDumping syndrome: keep food IN
Heartburn, regurgitation · small meals · sit up 1 hr after eating · head of bed (HOB) up · lose weight After gastric surgery: weakness, palpitations, diarrhea (early 20–30 min; late 1–3 hr = low glucose) · lie on the left side after meals (Iggy: lie down, no side named) · no fluids with meals · low carb, high protein and fat

Bowel: inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), ostomies, appendicitis

  • IBD Ulcerative colitis = large intestine only, cured by colectomy · Crohn's = mostly ileum, anywhere; avoid surgery. Low-residue diet in flares.
  • Colonoscopy Clear liquids 12–24 hr · ice-cold percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) prep until clear · nothing by mouth (NPO) 6–8 hr · after: watch for perforation.
  • Ostomies Ileostomy = liquid, never irrigated, dehydration and kidney stones · colostomy farther down = more formed; irrigate descending/sigmoid only, same time daily, left side. Healthy stoma = pink and moist.
  • Appendicitis Pain first, then nausea and vomiting (N/V); right lower quadrant (RLQ) (McBurney point). #1 worry = rupture: no enemas, laxatives, or heat. After surgery: Fowler's.

Total parenteral nutrition (TPN) and central lines

  • TPN Central line, filter, pump, dedicated line · bag hangs 24 hr max, new tubing each bag · glucose every 6 hr · daily weight · taper off · infection = #1 complication. Refeeding syndrome (low phosphorus and potassium (K)) → call.
  • Central lines Insert in Trendelenburg · chest x-ray before use · tubing change: clamp or Valsalva · air embolism → left side, Trendelenburg.

Kid version

Pancreatitis is the pancreas eating itself with its own soap, and every meal squirts more soap. The liver is a cleaning factory; when it's scarred, ammonia piles up and fogs the brain, and traffic backs up onto thin side roads (varices) that can burst.

Thyroid

Hyperthyroid (Graves): gas pedal stuckHypothyroid: foot off the gas
Hot, sweaty, weight loss, fast heart rate (HR), exophthalmos · thyroxine (T₄) up, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) down Cold, tired, weight gain, slow heart rate (HR), constipated · thyroxine (T₄) down, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) up · often has coronary artery disease (CAD)
  • Hyper meds Methimazole (infection risk, birth defects) · propylthiouracil (PTU) (liver toxicity) · saturated solution of potassium iodide (SSKI) in juice with a straw, 1 hr after the antithyroid drug · propranolol for symptoms · radioactive iodine (RAI): rule out pregnancy, no babies or kissing for 1 week, becomes hypothyroid.
  • Thyroid storm Fever, heart rate (HR) way up, agitation → airway, antithyroid drug then iodide, propranolol, cooling + acetaminophen (no aspirin), steroids. Never palpate the gland.
  • Levothyroxine Empty stomach, 30–60 min before breakfast; separate from calcium and iron by 4 hr; start low (CAD). Chest fullness before a dose → hold and call.
  • Myxedema coma Level of consciousness (LOC) way down, low temp, heart rate (HR), blood pressure (BP), sodium (Na), glucose → airway first, intravenous (IV) thyroid hormone, warm blankets; avoid sedatives.
  • Thyroidectomy Priority = hemorrhage: check the dressing and behind the neck. Then airway (trach set at bedside), hoarseness (nerve damage), hypocalcemia (tingling, Chvostek). Head of bed (HOB) up.

Parathyroid and adrenal medulla

  • Parathyroid Secretes parathyroid hormone (PTH), which pulls calcium from bone. Hyper = calcium (Ca) up, phosphorus down, "sedated," kidney stones, fragile bones. Hypo = Ca down, phosphorus up, "not sedated" (twitching, tetany) → intravenous (IV) calcium.
  • Pheochromocytoma Epinephrine bursts: headache, fast heart rate (HR), sweating, high blood pressure (BP). 24-hr urine (vanillylmandelic acid (VMA), metanephrines). Don't palpate the abdomen. Surgery.

Steroid rule Never stop abruptly · more during illness · vomiting 24–36 hr → intramuscular (IM) hydrocortisone and emergency department (ED).

Diabetes

  • Types Type 1 = no insulin, abrupt, first sign may be diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) · Type 2 = resistance, gradual · Gestational = screen at 24–28 weeks.
  • Diagnosis (2 abnormal tests) glycated hemoglobin (A1C) ≥ 6.5% · fasting glucose ≥ 126 · oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) ≥ 200. Goal A1C ≤ 7%.
  • Diet and exercise Plate method · high fiber · exercise at the same time daily when glucose is highest; eat first.
  • Metformin No hypoglycemia alone; hold for contrast, restart after 48 hr if creatinine is normal. Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) drugs: report severe pain to the back (pancreatitis).
  • Insulin Rapid (lispro) within 10 min of a meal · regular = the intravenous (IV) insulin · neutral protamine Hagedorn (NPH) cloudy, peaks 6–14 hr · glargine: no peak, never mix or give IV · clear before cloudy. Nothing by mouth (NPO): still give basal, hold mealtime insulin. Sick: never skip insulin.

Kid version

Insulin is the key that opens the cell door so sugar can get in, and potassium sneaks in through the same door. That's why fixing diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) with insulin can drop potassium fast. Addison = not enough steroid, so everything runs low except potassium. Cushing = too much, so you get a puffy "cushion" body that catches infections.

Orders and positions to know

Put-in-order practice

Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

  1. Normal saline (NS) first
  2. Intravenous (IV) regular insulin
  3. Hourly glucose and potassium (K)
  4. Add 5% dextrose in water (D5W) at 250–300

High potassium (K) with electrocardiogram (ECG) changes

  1. Intravenous (IV) calcium gluconate
  2. Insulin + glucose
  3. Potassium (K) binder
  4. Diuretic or dialysis

Chest pain at home

  1. 1 nitroglycerin (NTG)
  2. Wait 5 min
  3. Not better → 911
  4. Repeat nitroglycerin (NTG) every 5 min (3 total)

Acute kidney injury (AKI) phases

  1. Initiation
  2. Oliguric
  3. Diuretic
  4. Recovery

Conscious hypoglycemia

  1. 15 g fast carb
  2. Recheck in 15 min
  3. Repeat if under 70
  4. Snack with protein

Colonoscopy prep

  1. Clear liquids 12–24 hr
  2. Prep until clear
  3. Nothing by mouth (NPO) 6–8 hr
  4. After: watch for perforation
