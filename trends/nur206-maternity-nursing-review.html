Objective 1SLO 1–4

Health care and health risks in the childbearing years

Kid version

Before and early in pregnancy, the baby is being built from scratch. Prenatal care is the building inspection: we check the "materials" (nutrition, vitamins), keep out things that wreck construction (alcohol, smoking, live vaccines, certain meds), and count how many builds mom has done before (GTPAL).

GTPAL and Naegele's rule

GTPAL
  • Gravida: every pregnancy, including the current one, twins count as 1
  • Term: births ≥37 wk
  • Preterm: births 20 to 36⁶⁄₇ wk
  • Abortions: losses <20 wk (spontaneous or induced)
  • Living children right now (twins = 2)
Naegele's rule (EDD)

LMP − 3 months + 7 days + 1 year

LMP June 10 → March 17 next year.

Always confirm whether the scenario says she is pregnant now. That changes G.

Signs of pregnancy

Presumptive (she reports)Probable (examiner finds)Positive (only the fetus)
Amenorrhea, N/V, breast tenderness, fatigue, urinary frequency, quickening Chadwick's (bluish cervix), Goodell's (soft cervix), Hegar's (soft lower uterus), + hCG test, ballottement, Braxton Hicks Fetal heart tones, fetal movement felt by examiner, fetus seen on ultrasound

Memory hook: Presumptive = Patient says. Probable = Practitioner finds. Positive = Proof of baby. A pregnancy test is only probable, because tumors like a molar pregnancy also make hCG.

Nutrition and teratogens

  • Folic acid 400–600 mcg: green leafy veg, fortified cereal, beans, citrus. Prevents spina bifida and anencephaly.
  • Iron: take with vitamin C (orange juice), not with milk, tea, or coffee. Expect black stools and constipation.
  • Vitamin A (retinol) in high doses is teratogenic; fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) build up.
  • Food safety: no raw fish/meat, unpasteurized dairy or soft cheese (Listeria), deli meat unless heated; limit high-mercury fish (shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish).
  • Weight gain (normal BMI): about 25–35 lb. Inadequate gain → risk of IUGR.
  • Vaccines: inactivated flu and Tdap are given in pregnancy. Live vaccines (MMR, varicella) are not; give them postpartum.
  • HIV testing is offered to every pregnant woman regardless of risk.
NCLEX traps
  • Miscarriage at 18 wk goes under A, not P.
  • A positive pregnancy test is probable, not positive.
  • Temp 100.2 °F at a prenatal visit is not normal pregnancy. Notify the provider.

Physiologic changes of pregnancy and family care

Kid version

Mom's body becomes a bigger delivery truck. She makes more blood (but the water goes up faster than the red cells, so labs look "anemic"), her heart pumps harder, and the heavy uterus can sit on the big vein in her back like a bowling ball on a garden hose. That's why we turn her on her left side.

Fetal circulation: three shortcuts

ShuntBypassesCloses when
Ductus venosusLiver (umbilical vein → IVC)Cord is clamped
Foramen ovaleLungs (right atrium → left atrium)Left atrial pressure rises above right after first breaths
Ductus arteriosusLungs (pulmonary artery → aorta)Gradually as oxygen rises, about 1–8 days; may stay open (PDA) after asphyxia
Analogy

Before birth the lungs and liver are closed stores, so blood takes detours (the shunts). At birth the stores open, the detour signs come down, and traffic goes the normal way. Venosus = liver, the two "arteriosus/ovale" ones = lungs.

Body system changes to know

  • Cardiovascular: blood volume up ~40–50%, HR up 10–15 bpm, cardiac output up. Physiologic anemia (dilution). Clotting factors rise, so DVT risk rises.
  • Supine hypotension: uterus compresses the vena cava → dizzy, pale, low BP, fetal distress. Fix: left lateral or a wedge under the right hip.
  • Respiratory: diaphragm pushed up, shortness of breath late in pregnancy is normal.
  • GI: progesterone relaxes smooth muscle → heartburn and constipation. Heartburn: small frequent meals, stay upright after eating.
  • Renal: frequency in 1st and 3rd trimesters; higher UTI risk.
  • Skin: linea nigra, chloasma, striae are normal.

Family care

  • Lamaze: patterned breathing and focal point, distraction. Bradley: partner-coached, deep relaxation, natural birth, abdominal breathing.
  • Expect ambivalence in the 1st trimester; it is normal.
  • Fathers/partners: engrossment (intense interest in the baby). Siblings: prepare them, expect regression.
NCLEX traps
  • "Reactive" NST is reassuring. Don't read it as a positive/bad test.
  • Low hemoglobin in pregnancy can be dilutional; true iron deficiency still gets treated.
  • Dizziness lying flat → reposition left side before anything else.

Labor, birth and postpartum assessment and interventions

Kid version

Labor is moving a passenger (baby) through a tunnel (pelvis) with an engine (contractions), and the person's mind matters too. After birth the uterus has to squeeze shut like a fist around open blood vessels. A soft uterus is a leaking faucet.

The Four P's

Powers

Contractions (frequency, duration, intensity) plus pushing.

Passage

Pelvis and soft tissue.

Passenger

Fetus (lie, presentation, position, station) and placenta.

Psyche

Anxiety and fear raise catecholamines and slow labor.

True vs false labor and the stages

True laborFalse labor
ContractionsRegular, closer, strongerIrregular, no pattern
WalkingMakes them strongerMay stop them
PainStarts in back, wraps to frontMostly front/abdomen
CervixChanges (dilates/effaces)No change. This is the deciding sign.
  • Stage 1: onset to full dilation (10 cm). Latent → active → transition (~8–10 cm, most intense). Longest stage, longer for nullipara.
  • Stage 2: full dilation to birth of baby.
  • Stage 3: baby to placenta. Shortest (~6 min). Separation signs: uterus becomes round, fundus rises, cord lengthens, gush of blood.
  • Stage 4: first 1–2 hours after placenta. VS, fundus, lochia q15min for the first hour. Highest hemorrhage risk.

LION PIT (lates, variables, prolonged)

Left side · Increase IV · O₂ · Notify · Pitocin off · Internal exam · Tocolytic

O₂ via non-rebreather 8–10 L. Tocolytic example: terbutaline 0.25 mg SQ.

Tachysystole on Pitocin

>5 contractions in 10 min, ctx >120 s, or <60 s rest.

Stop Pitocin, side-lying, bolus ≥500 mL, O₂, notify, anticipate terbutaline. Restart at ½ the previous rate when resolved.

Scalp stimulation is used to elicit an acceleration, but never during a deceleration. Wait for the decel to resolve.

Pain management: epidural

  • Before: baseline VS, IV access, preload 500–1000 mL LR, position sitting or side-lying.
  • Test dose: watch for intravascular or spinal injection (metallic taste, mouth numbness, tinnitus, sudden dense block, hypotension).
  • After: BP q5min × 15 min; RN stays in room 30 min. Check FHR, block level, bladder (she can't feel it).
  • Hypotension (usually first 15 min): reposition, fluid bolus, O₂, ephedrine.
  • Spinal headache: worse upright. Bed rest, fluids, blood patch.
  • Contraindications: coagulopathy, uncorrected hypovolemia, infection at site or severe systemic infection, allergy, fetal condition needing immediate birth.

NCLEX traps
  • Early decels need nothing. Don't pick "turn off Pitocin" for earlies.
  • Boggy uterus: massage first, meds second. Displaced uterus: empty the bladder first.
  • Never push on a soft uterus to express clots; risk of inversion.
  • Methergine is held for hypertension, so check BP first.

Newborn needs: physiology, behavior, nutrition

Kid version

A newborn is a tiny wet phone with a small battery. It loses heat fast, burns sugar to stay warm (brown fat), and if it gets cold its battery (glucose) drains and it starts breathing hard. Keep it dry, warm, and fed, and the rest usually takes care of itself.

Blue hands and feet (acrocyanosis) = 1, not 2. That's why most healthy newborns get 9, not 10. Scoring does not predict long-term outcome.

Thermoregulation

Evaporation (biggest at birth)Wet skin, bathDry immediately, remove wet blanket, hat

Cold stress chain: cold → brown fat burned (nonshivering thermogenesis) → more O₂ and glucose used → hypoglycemia, respiratory distress, metabolic acidosis. Newborns cannot shiver effectively.

Glucose and bilirubin

Hypoglycemia

At risk: IDM, preterm, post-term, SGA/LGA, cold stress, maternal terbutaline.

Signs: jitteriness/tremors, poor feeding, sweating, temp drop, resp distress, high-pitched cry.

≤40–45 and no symptoms → feed now, recheck 30–60 min. Symptomatic or very low → notify, anticipate IV dextrose.

Jaundice

Physiologic: appears day 2–3, falls by day 5–7. Treat with frequent feeding (bilirubin leaves in stool).

Pathologic: in the first 24 hr. Common cause Rh/ABO incompatibility. Phototherapy. Risk: kernicterus.

Moves head → toes. Check by blanching nose or sternum in natural light.

Routine care and meds

  • Vitamin K IM (vastus lateralis): newborn gut is sterile, so it can't make vitamin K yet. Prevents hemorrhagic disease.
  • Erythromycin eye ointment: prevents gonorrheal/chlamydial eye infection.
  • Hep B vaccine before discharge, parental consent required. Mom HBsAg+ → vaccine + HBIG within 12 hr, separate sites.
  • First void within 24 hr, meconium within 24–48 hr. Cord: keep dry and clean, falls off in 1–2 weeks.
  • Back to sleep. Car seat rear-facing.
  • Caput succedaneum crosses suture lines, present at birth. Cephalohematoma does not cross sutures, appears in 1–2 days, raises jaundice risk.

Feeding

  • Breastfeed in the first hour, then 8–12 times a day. No water or glucose water.
  • Good latch: lips flanged, much of the areola in, audible swallowing, no nipple pain.
  • Adequate intake: 6 wet diapers by day 4, regains birth weight by 2 weeks.
  • Engorgement: feed more often (every 1.5–2 hr), warm shower/massage before feeding, supportive bra.
  • Sore nipples: fix the latch, start on the less sore side, no soap on nipples.
  • Mom drinks to thirst. Forcing fluids does not raise supply; frequent emptying does.
NCLEX traps
  • Jaundice at 18 hours is never "normal."
  • Tremors with a normal glucose → think NAS or hypocalcemia, not hypoglycemia.
  • Periodic breathing (pauses <20 s, no color/HR change) is normal. Apnea ≥20 s or with color/HR change is not.

Complications of childbearing

Bleeding in early pregnancy

Ectopic pregnancy

Implants outside uterus, usually tube. Risks: prior ectopic, PID, tubal surgery/scarring.

Signs: one-sided pelvic pain, spotting, shoulder pain (blood irritating diaphragm), shock if ruptured.

Intact tube: methotrexate. Ruptured: salpingectomy. Priority: bleeding and shock.

Hydatidiform mole

Grape-like trophoblastic tissue, no fetus. Premalignant (choriocarcinoma).

Uterus too big for dates, very high hCG, severe N/V, preeclampsia before 24 wk, hyperthyroid.

D&C, then serial hCG for 6–12 months. No pregnancy during follow-up.

Analogy

Previa is a rug laid over the doorway: as the door opens, the rug tears and bleeds, but nothing is pulling on it, so no pain. Abruption is wallpaper ripped off the wall from behind: it hurts, blood gets trapped behind it, and the wall goes rock hard.

Hypertensive disorders

  • Preeclampsia: new HTN ≥140/90 after 20 wk + proteinuria or end-organ signs. Watch for headache, visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain, hyperreflexia, edema, rapid weight gain.
  • Severe features: ≥160/110, persistent headache, scotomata, RUQ pain, pulmonary edema, UO <30 mL/hr, low platelets, high liver enzymes.
  • Treatment: magnesium sulfate (prevents seizures; not an antihypertensive), labetalol or hydralazine for BP, delivery is the cure.
  • HELLP: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets (<100,000). RUQ pain, N/V, "flu-like." Don't palpate the liver (rupture risk).
  • Eclampsia: seizure. Side-lying, protect airway, don't restrain, O₂, Mg.
  • Preeclampsia can appear postpartum (up to weeks later): headache not relieved by meds, flashing lights → come in.
Magnesium, kid version

Mag is a dimmer switch for the nerves. It turns the brain down so it can't seize. Turn it too far and the reflexes go out first, then breathing, then the heart. So you check the knee reflex, count breaths, and measure urine (kidneys clear it). Calcium gluconate turns the lights back up.

Toxicity order: DTRs lost → RR <12 → cardiac arrest

Diabetes, Rh, infection

  • GDM: insulin needs drop fast after the placenta delivers (it made the insulin-resistance hormones). Lifelong higher type 2 risk; needs follow-up screening.
  • Infant of diabetic mother: macrosomia (fetal insulin acts like growth hormone) → shoulder dystocia, birth injury. Hypoglycemia lowest at 1–3 hr. Also RDS (insulin delays surfactant), polycythemia → jaundice, hypocalcemia. Long-standing maternal vascular disease can instead cause IUGR.
  • Rh: mom Rh−, baby Rh+. First baby usually fine; antibodies hit later pregnancies. RhoGAM at 28 wk, within 72 hr after birth, and after any bleeding event, trauma, amnio/CVS, miscarriage, ectopic. It protects future pregnancies. Indirect Coombs = mom's blood; direct Coombs = baby's.
  • GBS: swab 35–37 wk; IV penicillin or ampicillin in labor q4h, at least 4 hr before birth. Treated in labor, not during pregnancy.
  • HIV in labor: avoid anything that breaks fetal skin (scalp electrode, forceps, vacuum, episiotomy). Epidural is fine. Bathe baby before injections. No breastfeeding (US guidance).

Preterm labor

  • Magnesium: short-term; main goal is neonatal neuroprotection (less CP, brain bleeds).
  • Nifedipine: calcium channel blocker. Headache, flushing, hypotension; report pulse >120.
  • Terbutaline: hold if maternal HR >120.
  • Corticosteroids (betamethasone) to mature fetal lungs.

Intrapartum emergencies

Cord prolapse

Call for help. Hips higher than head (knee-chest or Trendelenburg). Gloved hand holds the presenting part off the cord until the OR. Emergency C/S.

Shoulder dystocia

"Turtle sign." Call for help, stop pushing, McRoberts (thighs to abdomen), suprapubic pressure. Never fundal pressure. Check baby's clavicles after.

Postpartum complications

  • PPH causes, the 4 T's: Tone (atony, #1), Trauma (lacerations, hematoma), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (clotting problems, DIC).
  • Risk factors: overdistended uterus (twins, macrosomia, polyhydramnios), grand multiparity (5+), prolonged or precipitous labor, forceps/vacuum, C/S, oxytocin or MgSO₄ use, general anesthesia, chorioamnionitis, previa/abruption/accreta, previous PPH.
  • Atony steps: massage → empty bladder → oxytocin → Methergine (not if HTN) / misoprostol / Hemabate (caution asthma; diarrhea common) → bimanual compression, balloon tamponade → surgery, hysterectomy last.
  • Fluids: large-bore IV, type & cross, keep UO ≥30 mL/hr.
  • DVT: unilateral calf pain, warmth, swelling. Don't massage the leg. PE: sudden dyspnea, chest pain, tachycardia → emergency.
  • Endometritis / infection: fever ≥100.4 °F after 24 hr, foul lochia, uterine tenderness. Mastitis: keep breastfeeding or pumping, antibiotics, rest.

NCLEX traps
  • Painless bright red bleeding at 32 wk: the wrong answer is "perform a vaginal exam."
  • Absent DTRs on magnesium → stop the infusion and notify, even if RR is still OK.
  • RhoGAM is not given if indirect Coombs is positive (already sensitized).
  • Shoulder pain + missed period + spotting = ectopic until proven otherwise.
Objective 6SLO 4

Nursing care plans

Kid version

A care plan is a recipe card: what's wrong (assessment and problem), what dish you're aiming for (outcome), the steps (interventions, each with a reason), and the taste test (evaluation). If it doesn't taste right, you change the recipe.

StepWhat you writeRule
AssessSubjective + objective dataReal numbers: "fundus boggy, 2 cm above umbilicus, deviated right"
DiagnosePatient problem + related to + as evidenced byNursing problem, not a medical diagnosis
PlanExpected outcomePatient-centered, measurable, time-limited
ImplementInterventions with rationalesAssess → act → teach; independent and collaborative
EvaluateWas the outcome met?Met / partly met / not met → revise

Worked example: postpartum hemorrhage from atony

  • Assessment: G3P3, 1 hr after vaginal birth of 4200 g baby. Fundus boggy, 2 cm above umbilicus. Saturating 1 pad in 15 min with clots. HR 112, BP 104/60.
  • Problem: Excessive blood loss related to uterine atony as evidenced by boggy fundus, saturating a pad in 15 minutes, and tachycardia.
  • Outcome: Fundus firm and midline, lochia ≤1 pad/hr, HR <100 within 1 hour.
  • Interventions:
    • Massage fundus while supporting lower uterine segment (stimulates contraction; support prevents inversion).
    • Empty bladder (full bladder pushes the uterus up and stops it from contracting).
    • Give uterotonics as ordered; check BP before Methergine.
    • Weigh pads (accurate quantitative blood loss).
    • Maintain large-bore IV, VS q15min, UO ≥30 mL/hr.
    • Call for help early; stay with the patient.
  • Evaluation: Fundus firm at U/U, 1 pad/hr, HR 92. Outcome met; continue q15min checks for the first hour.

Active recall

Self-check

Answer in your head before you open each one. If you miss it, go back to the section and find the line yourself.

01Pregnant now. History: twins born at 35 wk (both living), one term birth, one miscarriage at 12 wk. GTPAL?

G4 T1 P1 A1 L3. Current pregnancy counts in G. Twins = one preterm birth but two living children.

02LMP was September 25. What is the EDD?

July 2 of next year. Sept − 3 = June; 25 + 7 = 32 → July 2.

03A client at 34 wk becomes pale and dizzy lying flat for her ultrasound. First action?

Turn her to her left side (or wedge the right hip). Supine hypotension from vena cava compression.

04NST: two accelerations of 15 bpm lasting 15 sec within 20 min. Interpret.

Reactive, which is reassuring. No further testing needed right now.

05On Pitocin, the strip shows decelerations that begin after the peak of each contraction. List actions in order.

Late decels → LION PIT. Stop Pitocin, left side, IV bolus, O₂ by non-rebreather, notify provider, anticipate tocolytic.

06Five minutes after an epidural dose, BP drops from 124/76 to 88/50. What do you do?

Reposition (side-lying/left uterine displacement), increase IV fluids, O₂, notify anesthesia, anticipate ephedrine. Check FHR.

07Two hours postpartum, the fundus is firm, 2 cm above the umbilicus, deviated to the right. Action?

Have her void (or catheterize), then reassess. Full bladder. Massage is for a boggy uterus.

08Fundus firm and midline, but a steady trickle of bright red blood continues. Most likely cause?

Laceration (cervix or vagina). Notify the provider. Massaging a firm uterus won't help.

09BP 150/100 postpartum with boggy uterus. Which uterotonic do you question?

Methylergonovine (Methergine). It vasoconstricts and raises BP. Hemabate is questioned in asthma.

10Client on magnesium: RR 14, UO 35 mL/hr, patellar reflex absent. Action?

Stop the infusion and notify. Loss of DTRs is the first sign of toxicity. Have calcium gluconate ready.

1136 wk, painless bright red bleeding, soft uterus. What must you not do?

Do not perform a vaginal exam. Suspected previa until ultrasound rules it out.

12Board-like abdomen, severe pain, dark bleeding, late decels. Priorities?

Abruption. Two large-bore IVs, type & cross, continuous FHR monitoring, watch for shock and DIC, prepare for emergency delivery.

13Mom is Rh−, baby is Rh+, indirect Coombs negative. When is RhoGAM due?

Within 72 hours of birth. It protects future pregnancies.

14Cord is felt in the vagina after ROM. First action?

Call for help and lift the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand; hips above head (knee-chest/Trendelenburg). Prepare for C/S. Don't push the cord back.

15Head delivers then retracts against the perineum. Which two nursing actions first?

McRoberts maneuver and suprapubic pressure (after calling for help). Never fundal pressure.

16Newborn at 1 min: HR 90, weak irregular breathing, cries when stimulated, moves all extremities, pink with blue hands/feet. APGAR?

7. HR 1, resp 1, reflex 2, tone 2, color 1. A 7 still gets reassessed at 5 minutes like every baby.

17Newborn of a diabetic mother is jittery at 2 hr. Glucose 38, no other symptoms. Action?

Feed now (breast or formula) and recheck in 30–60 min. Notify if symptomatic or not improving.

18Jaundice noted at 16 hours of life. Physiologic or pathologic?

Pathologic (first 24 hr). Get a bilirubin level, notify, think Rh/ABO incompatibility.

19Why does a newborn get vitamin K?

The newborn gut is sterile, so it has no bacteria to make vitamin K yet. Prevents hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.

20Day 4 postpartum: tearful, irritable, but caring for baby. Day 21: same symptoms plus can't sleep when baby sleeps, feels worthless. Difference?

Day 4 = blues (normal, support). Day 21 = possible postpartum depression (>2 wk) → screen with EPDS and refer.

21HIV-positive client in labor. Which is acceptable: fetal scalp electrode, epidural, vacuum, episiotomy?

Epidural. The other three break the fetal skin or increase exposure to maternal blood.
